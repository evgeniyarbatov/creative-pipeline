This one's from the bamboo forest section near the end of the Sapa run — sorry, Sapa, Vietnam, not Sapam, I always say that wrong.

So by that point I was like 90-something kilometers in, maybe 95, I don't actually remember the exact number, and there's this really steep climb through dense bamboo, like clusters of it, twenty, thirty trees close together, packed in tight.

It felt dark and cold in there even though I think it was actually morning, the trees just blocked everything out. I remember feeling kind of lost, like disoriented, like the whole earth was just that one hill and nothing else existed.

And honestly it was miserable, my legs were done, I wanted to stop so bad — but also it was the calmest part of the whole run, weirdly. Everything slowed down, I wasn't racing anymore, I was just moving through it, like I was barely even running, more like walking with intention.

I don't know if "cherishing" is the right word but I keep coming back to that feeling of wanting to hold onto it because it was so different from everything else in the race. I drew this a few weeks after, when I was already back home, trying to get back that stillness.

There's a smell thing too — wet leaves, kind of sweet, it's been bugging me, I can't place where else I've smelled that. Might be nothing.
