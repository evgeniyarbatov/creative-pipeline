There’s a particular stillness that settles over you in places like Sapam, Vietnam. I was running a
100-kilometer race, and a significant portion was spent winding through these incredibly dense
bamboo forests. The trees were so close together – often in groups of thirty or more – that it felt
like walking through a dark, cool tunnel. Seeing the shadows shift and the way the light filtered
through the stalks created this incredible disorientation; it was as if the entire landscape was
just a steep hill, a constant, deliberate pace.

It wasn't about conquering the distance, but about allowing myself to truly *observe*. These
forests, unexpectedly, prompted a necessary pause. They offered a moment for reflection, a time to
simply *be* within that quietness. It’s rare to find such a profound shift in perspective amidst the
demands of a run. I wanted to capture that feeling – that sense of slowing down, of appreciation for
the raw, unyielding beauty surrounding me. It’s always delightful to encounter them, and it feels
like it feels like rest to walk slowly among them. They give you pause, they get, it’s a time to
reflect, time to think. It’s one of those moments I want to cherish.
