# Philosophy

Creative Pipeline preserves what an artist was thinking when they made a piece. It does not produce captions, tags, or descriptions meant for an audience. See `ROADMAP.md` for the full direction.

## Principles

### 1. Preserve voice

Favor the artist's phrasing over paraphrase. When in doubt, quote or stay close to the spoken line.

- **Good:** `"the lines are wiggly because they never sit still"`
- **Bad:** "The artwork features dynamic, organic linework."

### 2. Keep contradiction

If the artist says two incompatible things, both belong in the output. Do not reconcile them.

- **Good:** `{"a": "it's about joy", "b": "it's kind of sad actually", "context": "said within a minute of each other"}`
- **Bad:** "The piece explores the bittersweet duality of joy and sadness." (invented synthesis, not something the artist said)

### 3. Keep uncertainty

"I think," "maybe," "I'm not sure," "or something like that" are signal, not noise.

- **Good:** `"not sure if the bottom should be darker"`
- **Bad:** "The darker base grounds the composition." (turns hesitation into a confident design statement)

### 4. Keep tangents

Side paths often hold the real memory. Tag them as tangents; do not delete them for focus.

- **Good:** `{"quote": "reminds me of being on the roof with my brother", "relation": "unclear — maybe color, maybe nothing"}`
- **Bad:** Omitting the tangent because it doesn't relate to the stated subject of the piece.

### 5. No invention

Nothing in the output that cannot be traced to the transcript. No added symbolism, backstory, or marketing framing.

- **Good:** Quoting the artist's own stated reason for making the piece.
- **Bad:** "This work speaks to the impermanence of the human condition." (nobody said this)

### 6. Reminder, not publication

Outputs are notes-to-future-self. Readable, but not performative.

- **Good:** A short fragment that jogs the artist's memory of the moment.
- **Bad:** A polished caption ready to paste into a social post.
